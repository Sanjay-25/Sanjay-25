Professional drone cleaning results come from professional chemistry. Not from the drone, not from pressure, and not from the equipment. Here is the complete framework for handling chemistry safely and responsibly on every commercial exterior cleaning job.. Sodium Hypochlorite Dilution: Getting the Concentration Right . The goal with sodium hypochlorite dilution is always to use the minimum effective concentration for the contamination and surface in front of you. Using more chemistry than the job requires does not produce better results. It increases surface damage risk, creates more runoff to manage, and raises cost without a corresponding benefit.. For heavy growth on brick, stone, or stucco, a concentration of approximately 4 percent sodium hypochlorite is the appropriate starting point. To achieve this from a 12.5 percent bleach concentrate, mix one part bleach to three parts water and apply with a soft wash system and surfactant. Allow 15 to 20 minutes of dwell time before rinsing. The dwell time is not optional. It is what gives the chemistry enough contact time to kill organic growth at the root level rather than just removing the surface appearance.. For light growth on siding, wood, or surfaces with less severe contamination, a concentration of 1 to 2 percent is typically appropriate. Mix one part 12.5 percent bleach to between 6 and 12 parts water depending on the severity of the contamination. Dwell time for light growth applications runs 10 to 15 minutes. Exact ratios will vary based on surface type, ambient temperature, and local practices. Always test in an inconspicuous area and dial in your mix before committing to a full application.. PPE: What Every Operator Needs on Every Job . Personal protective equipment is not optional and it is not just about regulatory compliance. It is about making sure every operator on the job site goes home the same way they arrived. The consequences of a chemical splash to the eyes or sustained respiratory exposure to sodium hypochlorite vapor are serious enough that no time saved by skipping a step is ever worth the risk.. Eye Protection . Chemical splash goggles are the minimum standard for any job involving sodium hypochlorite or other cleaning chemicals. A full face shield is required for overhead work where chemical drift and drip are more likely. Standard safety glasses are not adequate protection for liquid chemical exposure.. Respiratory Protection . An N95 respirator is the minimum for outdoor applications with adequate ventilation. Chemical cartridge respirators are required for enclosed or poorly ventilated areas and for applications involving heavy mist or prolonged exposure. A full-face respirator is appropriate for extensive exposure scenarios. Match the respirator to the actual exposure level rather than defaulting to the minimum.. Skin Protection . Chemical-resistant gloves in nitrile or neoprene are required for all chemical handling. Long sleeves and pants protect skin from incidental contact and drift. A chemical-resistant apron is appropriate for mixing operations where spill risk is higher. Closed-toe shoes with chemical resistance protect against ground-level spills and runoff.. Landscaping Protection: Before, During, and After . Chemical runoff and drift are among the most common sources of client complaints and liability exposure in exterior cleaning. Landscaping damage is visible, emotional, and memorable in a way that property managers do not forget. A thorough protection protocol eliminates that risk and demonstrates a level of professionalism that most competitors do not bother to show.. Before the Job . Identify all sensitive plants, ornamental shrubs, and ground cover in the treatment zone before setting up. Pre-water all vegetation thoroughly with clean water before any chemistry is applied. Saturating plants before exposure dilutes any chemical contact that occurs and significantly reduces uptake. Cover delicate plants with plastic sheeting where practical. Identify the location of all storm drains in or adjacent to the treatment area and protect them if runoff could reach them during the application.. During the Job . Monitor wind direction continuously throughout the application. Chemical drift from drone operations at height can carry further than operators expect, particularly in light crosswinds that are not strong enough to suspend operations. Use targeted application patterns that keep overspray away from landscaping and unintended surfaces. Keep a continuous water supply available throughout the job for immediate dilution response if drift occurs. Adjust drone positioning and application angles when wind conditions shift.. After the Job . Rinse all vegetation in the treatment zone immediately after the application is complete, regardless of whether visible chemical contact occurred during the job. Remove any protective coverings once the chemistry has been neutralized or rinsed away. Monitor plant health for 24 to 48 hours after completion and document your observations. If a client contacts you about plant stress during that window, a documented post-job monitoring record turns a potential dispute into a manageable conversation.. Chemical Neutralization Basics . Sodium hypochlorite neutralizes naturally over time with dilution and exposure to sunlight. For most soft wash applications on exterior surfaces, thorough rinsing and natural neutralization are sufficient. Acid-based cleaners require specific neutralizing agents that must be confirmed before the job and applied deliberately after cleaning. Never leave a job site with active chemistry on a surface or in runoff channels without confirming neutralization. Provide the client with post-cleaning care instructions that cover any monitoring they should do in the hours following the job.. Documentation: Your Protection When Questions Arise . Even when a job is executed flawlessly, clients sometimes follow up with concerns about plant health or surface appearance in the days after cleaning. Documentation is what protects operators in those conversations. Log the chemicals used, the concentrations applied, and the surfaces treated on every job. Note the landscaping protection steps taken and the condition of the plants at the time of the post-treatment rinse. If you monitored plant health in the 24 to 48 hours after completion, record what you observed. Soft Wash vs Pressure Wash vs Window Cleaning: Choosing the Right Method Every Time . Drone cleaning does not change what cleans a surface. It changes how safely and efficiently cleaning solutions reach one. The three fundamental exterior cleaning methods still apply to every job, and using the wrong one is one of the most expensive mistakes an operator can make. Here is what each method does, how it works, and when to use it.. Window Cleaning . Window cleaning is designed for glass and glazed surfaces where streak-free results are non-negotiable. The process applies a specialized cleaning solution that breaks down contamination on the glass surface, then rinses with ultra-pure filtered water to prevent mineral deposits and spotting. The critical variable is not pressure. It is water quality. Water that has not been filtered to near-zero parts per million will leave mineral deposits on glass regardless of how well the chemical step performs. Operating pressure for window cleaning typically runs between 800 and 1,200 PSI, which is enough to rinse effectively without risking seal damage or thermal stress on the glass.. The most common mistakes on glass are using unfiltered water, using incorrect chemical ratios, and rushing the rinse step. All three produce spotting and streaking that customers notice immediately and remember for a long time. Streak-free glass comes from chemistry and water quality, not force.. Soft Washing . Soft washing is the correct method for mold, mildew, algae, and organic growth on surfaces that cannot handle high pressure. Stucco, EIFS, painted wood, certain brick types, and most roofing materials all fall into this category. The process applies a chemical solution, typically sodium hypochlorite combined with a surfactant for cling and dwell performance, at low pressure and allows it to sit on the surface for 10 to 15 minutes. During that dwell window, the chemistry kills the organic growth at the root level rather than simply removing what is visible on the surface.. This root-level kill is what makes soft washing results last significantly longer than pressure washing on organically contaminated surfaces. A pressure wash removes the visible growth without treating the root system, which means regrowth begins almost immediately. A soft wash removes both the visible growth and the biological structure underneath it, which extends the clean result substantially. Operating pressure stays under 300 PSI with a flow rate around 10 to 12 gallons per minute. Surface damage risk at those parameters is minimal when dilution is correct and dwell time is respected.. Pressure Washing . Pressure washing uses mechanical force to remove contamination from hard, durable surfaces that can tolerate the pressure involved. It produces immediate visual results and works well on concrete, pavers, and certain metal surfaces. Operating pressure can reach up to 4,400 PSI with flow rates under 8 gallons per minute. The results are visually satisfying and the process is fast.. The limitation is that pressure washing works at the surface level only. It removes what is visible without addressing the biology underneath, which means organic growth returns faster than it would after a soft wash treatment. Pressure washing is the right tool for the right surface and the right contamination type. It is not the right default for every job, and applying it to surfaces that require soft washing can cause real and expensive damage.. Surface by Surface: Matching Method to Material . Glass and Windows . Glass requires window cleaning with filtered water and a specialized cleaning solution at 800 to 1,200 PSI. The single most important variable is water purity. Nothing else matters if the rinse water is leaving mineral deposits. Use deionized or reverse osmosis filtered water and confirm the output reads as close to zero parts per million as possible before starting.. Stucco and EIFS . Stucco and EIFS are among the most damage-prone surfaces in commercial exterior cleaning. They require soft washing at very low pressure, under 200 PSI, with sodium hypochlorite and a surfactant mix that allows proper dwell time. High pressure on these materials strips finish coats, forces water behind the cladding system, and voids manufacturer warranties. There is no job on stucco or EIFS where high pressure is the right answer.. Brick and Masonry . Brick can typically tolerate soft washing or light pressure washing in the 200 to 800 PSI range, but it always deserves a test patch first. Older mortar can be fragile and may not survive even moderate pressure. Acid-based cleaners on brick require careful testing because the wrong acid at the wrong concentration causes permanent discoloration and surface damage. When in doubt, start with soft washing and only introduce pressure if the surface clearly needs it.. Metal Panels . Metal panel cleaning varies significantly based on the finish type and the nature of the contamination. Low to moderate pressure applies in most cases. The critical rule is to avoid chemicals that react with the specific metal finish in use. Sodium hypochlorite on certain metal finishes causes oxidation and discoloration that cannot be reversed. Confirm the panel finish type before selecting chemistry and test in an inconspicuous area first when working with an unfamiliar material.. Concrete . Concrete is the most forgiving surface in exterior cleaning and can handle pressure washing up to around 3,000 PSI in most applications. Soft washing is also effective on concrete when organic growth is the primary contamination type. The key consideration for concrete is sealer compatibility. Decorative concrete finishes and sealed surfaces can be damaged by the wrong chemistry or excessive pressure, so confirm what has been applied to the surface before selecting your approach.. The Core Rule . Match the method to the contamination type and the surface material, not to the equipment you happen to have available. When the contamination is organic growth of any kind, mold, algae, mildew, moss, or lichen, chemistry is the primary weapon. Pressure without chemistry removes the surface appearance of organic growth without treating the biology underneath it. The result comes back faster, the customer is disappointed sooner, and the operator's reputation takes the hit.. Drone cleaning rewards method knowledge because it makes professional execution accessible on surfaces and heights where traditional methods create too much risk or too much disruption. The technology advantage is real. But it only delivers its full value when the operator behind it knows which chemistry to apply, at what pressure, on which surface, for how long.. The Big Takeaway . Soft washing, pressure washing, and window cleaning each have a purpose and a set of surfaces they are designed for. Choosing correctly every time is what separates operators who build long-term client relationships from those who cause damage and lose accounts. Learn the methods. Know the surfaces. The FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate is earned through a written knowledge test administered at an FAA-approved testing center. The exam consists of 60 multiple-choice questions covering aeronautical knowledge topics relevant to unmanned aircraft operations. A passing score is 70 percent, and there is no flight test component. Most operators who prepare systematically are ready to sit for the exam within two to three weeks using a combination of FAA study guides, online prep courses, and practice exams. The exam is not trivial, but it is not designed to be prohibitive either. It is designed to ensure that commercial operators have a working understanding of airspace, weather, and operational safety before they fly for hire.. Once earned, the Remote Pilot Certificate must be renewed every 24 months through an online recurrent training course. The renewal process is significantly lighter than the initial certification and can be completed in a few hours.. Weight Limits That Apply to Drone Cleaning Systems . Part 107 operations are limited to aircraft with a maximum takeoff weight of 55 pounds. For drone cleaning systems, this calculation is more complex than it is for standard drone operations because the total system weight includes the drone itself, the cleaning payload, the hose, and the weight of any water or chemical solution in the system at the time of flight. All of those components count toward the 55-pound limit, and operators who have not run these numbers carefully can find themselves in violation without realizing it.. Weight calculations for your specific system should be documented before operations begin and revisited any time you change equipment, add a payload, or modify your hose configuration. This documentation protects you if you are ever questioned about compliance, and it demonstrates the kind of operational rigor that serious commercial customers expect from professional vendors.. The Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability system, commonly called LAANC, allows operators to request airspace authorization through FAA-approved apps and often receive approval in seconds for altitudes within pre-defined grids. Many commercial cleaning altitudes fall within auto-approval parameters, which means operators who are prepared can obtain authorization on site before a job without any meaningful delay.
