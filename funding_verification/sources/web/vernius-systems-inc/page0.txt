The vast majority of interceptors are still manually piloted. . Active radar seekers would enable any interceptor to launch autonomously, but at six figures per unit they cost significantly more than the targets they are meant to intercept. . 50%+. of Shaheds over Ukraine are now jet-powered . C2 latency forces FPV operators to manually locate targets in the last kilometer , and jet Shaheds are too fast to catch. . . $4M. per Patriot intercept. The single most expensive subsystem of every autonomous interceptor is its radar seeker head . . . . . . Capabilities . Beyond-visual-range guidance.. Autonomous or operator-assisted. . The same seeker supports two modes of employment. It can carry an interceptor to the target on its own after launch, or feed live target telemetry back to a pilot flying manually — closing the gap between the cueing network and visual acquisition. . . . Mode 01 . Fully Autonomous Interception . . . . The interceptor launches on a fire control cue from the C2 network and flies out under radar guidance. The seeker acquires the target beyond visual range and hands off to terminal guidance for the final intercept — no pilot in the loop. . C2 Fire Control → Launch → Radar Midcourse → Terminal Seeker . . . . . Mode 02 . Operator-Assisted Guidance . . . . For crews flying manually, the seeker streams live target range and bearing back down the link. The pilot sees the target on their display long before it is visible — at distance, at night, and through fog, smoke, and rain. . Radar Track → Live Telemetry → Pilot Display . . . . . . . .
