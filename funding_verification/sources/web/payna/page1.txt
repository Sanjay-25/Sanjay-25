WHY WE EXIST. Permission is infrastructure. It has rarely been built like it.. Financial services in the United States are licensed state by state. A company that wants to lend, insure, collect, or move money in more than one place does not apply once. It applies everywhere, to different regulators, on different forms, against different net worth and bonding rules, and then it does the whole thing again every renewal cycle for as long as it operates.. That work is real and it is constant, and almost nowhere is it anyone’s actual job. It gets absorbed by a general counsel, an operations lead, or a founder, tracked in a spreadsheet, and remembered through a calendar reminder somebody set two years ago. It holds until it does not. The failure is rarely dramatic. It is a lapsed license in a state the company had stopped thinking about, found during an exam.. We think that is a systems problem rather than a staffing one. The rules are written down. The deadlines are knowable. The filings are repetitive. What has been missing is somewhere for all of it to live at once, so that the state of a company’s permission to operate is a thing you can look at rather than a thing you have to reconstruct.. Payna is that place. We build the licensing and compliance layer for regulated finance, and we treat it the way you would treat any other piece of production infrastructure: monitored, versioned, auditable, and boring on purpose.. . . . WHAT WE BELIEVE. Six positions that shape what we build.. INFRASTRUCTURE [1] . A license is not paperwork. It is the thing that lets a company exist in a state. Treated as filing work it becomes a scramble every renewal season. Treated as infrastructure it becomes something you can plan against.. . HUMAN SIGN-OFF [2] . A person signs every filing. Our models read documents, draft applications, and cross-check requirements. They do not submit. A person reviews what reaches a regulator, and you decide where that line sits.. . ONE SYSTEM [3] . Fragmentation is the actual problem. Every state has its own forms, portals, fees, and clocks, and some cities have their own on top. Split that across spreadsheets and inboxes and the deadline you miss is the one nobody owned.. . THE RECORD [4] . Compliance is worth what it can prove. An examiner will eventually ask when something happened and who approved it. Every action is logged, timestamped, and reviewable, because answering that question later is the job.. . PEOPLE TOO [5] . Companies hold licenses, and so do people. Loan originators, producers, and adjusters carry their own credentials, sponsorships, and continuing education. A system that tracks only the entity is half a system.. . QUIET [6] . The goal is for none of this to be interesting. Nobody wants licensing to be exciting. The measure of the work is that renewals pass without anyone noticing and nothing arrives in front of a regulator that should not have.. . . . . WHO WE BUILD FOR.
