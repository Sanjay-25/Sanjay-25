The bet: hotels are the original AI use case, and the operating system should be a 24/7 workforce, not a phone tree. Three production systems are already in hotels: Lance Live (voice), the sales email agent, and operations / the daily manager report.. . The hotel is the original AI use case.. Hotels are extraordinary businesses. A modest 100-room property handles thousands of guest interactions a week — wake-up calls, housekeeping requests, late check-ins, lost room keys, dinner recommendations, group inquiries, maintenance issues, pre-arrival questions, post-departure folios. Each interaction is small. Together, they are the entire experience. And yet most of these interactions still happen the way they did in 1985. A guest dials zero. The phone rings at the front desk. A staff member, already mid-checkout, picks up. A note gets written down. A radio call goes out. Sometimes the request makes it to the right person; sometimes it gets lost in a shift change. Email goes to a shared inbox no one truly owns. Group inquiries languish for days. Maintenance tickets live in three different systems. The result is a structural problem the industry has lived with for decades: the people closest to the guest are the most overloaded. Front desk agents context-switch hundreds of times a shift. Housekeeping leaders spend their day chasing rooms instead of running them. General managers find themselves answering emails at 11pm because the day was consumed by interruptions. The labor that gets squeezed is the labor that creates the guest experience.. . Why we started Lance.. Lance started with a simple question: if we were designing a hotel today, from scratch, with the AI tools that exist in 2026 — what would the operating system look like? It wouldn't look like a phone tree. It wouldn't look like a shared inbox. It wouldn't look like an IVR menu that asks the caller to press 4 for the spa. It would look like a workforce — autonomous, multilingual, available 24/7 — that knows the property, knows the guest, and knows the systems. It would pick up on the first ring. It would understand the question. It would do the work. That's what we've spent the last year building. Not a chatbot. Not a single feature bolted onto a PMS. A new layer of infrastructure for hotels — a layer where agents handle the conversational and operational work that humans were never supposed to do.. . What we've shipped: Lance Live, the voice layer.. Lance today is three production systems working in concert, deployed in real hotels handling real guests. The first is Lance Live, our voice agent. It answers the hotel's main line in under a second, handles the call end-to-end, and routes anything it can't resolve to the right department with full context — not a cold transfer. In production, Lance Live is taking calls in multiple languages, booking wake-up calls, dispatching housekeeping and maintenance requests, and answering the questions that make a hotel feel like home — pool hours, parking, breakfast, the fact that the hot tub closes at 9pm on Sundays. When a guest needs a human, the handoff is instant and the receiving team gets full context.. . The Sales Email Agent: the inbox layer.. Group sales is one of the highest-margin and worst-served workflows in hospitality. A wedding planner sends an inquiry. A corporate travel manager asks about a 40-room block. The response time at most hotels is measured in days. Lance's sales email agent reads every inquiry the moment it lands, drafts a tailored response with the right rate, the right cutoff date, the right group concessions, and submits it for a one-click human approval. Approved replies go out in minutes — not Monday morning. Conversations stay threaded. Pre-arrival checklists, upsell campaigns, and group briefs run on the same rails.. . Operations and the Daily Manager Report.. The third surface is operational: schedules, work orders, preventive maintenance, readings, announcements, multi-language messaging — the full back-of-house. Lance generates next-week labor schedules tailored to forecast occupancy and the rules of each property. Every morning, supervisors at every Lance hotel get a briefing email with last night's calls, today's arrivals, the labor plan, and anything that needs their attention — and they can reply in plain English to ask follow-up questions.. . Why now.. Three things are true at the same time, and we don't think they'll be true together for very long. Voice models finally crossed the line. The latency, naturalness, and reasoning of modern speech-native models is — for the first time — indistinguishable from a human on the other end of a phone for the kinds of interactions a hotel handles. Two years ago this was science fiction. Today it's a config file. Hotels are short on people. Post-pandemic staffing levels never came back. The industry is operating 10–15% leaner than 2019, with no realistic path to hire its way out. Every hotel we've spoken with — luxury, select-service, full-service, independent, branded — is making the same tradeoff: which guest interactions don't get answered today. The integrations are finally accessible. The systems hotels run on are opening up, and AI agents can now operate the ones that aren't. The integration tax that killed every previous attempt at hotel automation has finally been paid down enough that a small team can ship real things. The window is now. We intend to use it.. . What customers are saying.. “Lance operates in a completely different league.
