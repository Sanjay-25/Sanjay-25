CTO, Metix AI . . . . . . . . . . Frequently asked questions. How is LightSprint different from Claude, Codex, or Cursor? . Claude, Codex, and Cursor are coding agents. LightSprint is the workspace that lets your entire product team use them together. . Instead of agent sessions living with one developer, LightSprint gives your team shared context, shared skills, cloud sandboxes, live previews, automated verification, and an approval workflow before anything ships. . You can keep using the models and coding tools you already like — LightSprint adds the collaboration and guardrails around them. . . . . Is LightSprint more expensive than using AI models directly? Can I use my existing subscription? . LightSprint doesn't mark up model inference. . Bring your own API keys or use existing Codex and Claude subscriptions, or choose another model through OpenRouter. You're paying LightSprint for the infrastructure around the models: cloud execution, shared context, previews, collaboration, testing, and team controls. . . . . How secure is LightSprint? What happens to our code and data? . Your team controls which repositories and models LightSprint can access. . Work runs in isolated cloud sandboxes, and those environments are destroyed after the work is complete. You choose the model provider, and LightSprint can also work with your own LLM gateway. . Every code change remains auditable through your existing GitHub workflow, and nothing can bypass the approval rules your engineering team configures. . . . . What tools does LightSprint support? Will it fit into our existing workflow? . LightSprint is built to work with the stack you already have rather than replace it. . Connect your existing codebase and repositories, keep your GitHub review and CI workflows, and use agents like Claude and Codex. Your team can also send work into LightSprint from tools such as Slack and Linear. . Engineering sets up the environment, shared skills, and guardrails once, then the rest of the team can build within those constraints. . . . . I'm not an engineer. Can I actually use LightSprint safely? . Yes — that's one of the main reasons LightSprint exists. . You describe what you want in plain English, plan the change visually, launch an agent, and review the result in a live sandbox without needing to understand Git or set up a development environment. . Non-engineers don't get a shortcut around engineering. Every change follows the approval workflow your team configures, so engineers still control what reaches production. . . . . . . . . Build it. .
