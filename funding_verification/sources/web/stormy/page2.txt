Our thesis: the command layer for physical operations. Software ate the office a long time ago. It never made it to the yard. The businesses that keep the physical world running — forklift fleets, HVAC crews, electricians, equipment dealers — still run on a ringing phone, a paper ticket and one dispatcher’s memory. Every piece of intelligence in the business lives in somebody’s head, and it goes home at six.. Physical operations need intelligence too. We are building for field work what Datadog is for software: one layer that sees every call, every truck, every part, every job and every invoice. And it does not stop at seeing. It answers, books, dispatches, orders and bills, with a person approving wherever it matters.. Build the API that runs physical-world operations. Why now. Two things changed. Models can now hold a phone call, read a photo of a fault code and write an invoice that is right. And the physical world is filling with machines — fleets, equipment, and soon robots — that all need servicing. Keeping them running is a harder problem than building them, and it is an operations problem: people, parts, trucks, schedules and money. That is the work Stormy runs.. We start at the front desk because that is where the work comes in. We earn the back office because that is where it gets lost. And we stay, because the layer that runs both becomes the one the whole operation runs on.. How we work. Everything in a service business that can be run by AI will be. Everything that matters is approved by a person. We build toward that one shop at a time, by four rules.. 01 Value lies in the extremes We build for the shops with the heaviest operations, not the average one. . 02 Act bold A real customer call on day one, never a sandbox. . 03 Run fast What we fix in one shop ships to every shop that week. . 04 Sell It is real when a shop pays for it. . We are forward-deployed. Palantir proved a boring, unglamorous thing: the hardest software problems are not solved by shipping a login page and waiting. They are solved by putting engineers inside the operation, on the floor, with the people doing the work. A live inbound call, a real dispatch, real parts off the shelf, a real invoice to a real customer — with you watching every step and able to stop it.. . . /03. We stay until the loop closes. Call → book → dispatch → parts → invoice → paid, end to end, without anybody in the office retyping it. That is the bar. We do not leave before it is met.. . . /04. What we learn ships to everyone.
