Our first partner: Jon Eberst. . 9:30 p.m. at a client. . Ryan at Demo Day. . Demo Day, between sessions. . . . Our vision. Injustice should never be administrative. . Administrative injustice looks like a records request sitting in a hospital queue for six weeks. A police report that only travels by mail. An adjuster who says Thursday and means nothing. Nobody ever decides that an injured person deserves less than they're owed. The clock just runs, the bills arrive faster than the paperwork, and one day the lowball offer stops looking like an insult and starts looking like rent. . That's how most justice is lost in America. Not in a courtroom. In a queue. . Plaintiff lawyers are the counterweight, and they're fighting with a hand tied. The best firms in the country still run intake on spreadsheets and staff entire rooms whose only job is to wait on hold with hospitals. A case that should take three weeks to work up takes four months. Winnable cases get turned away for lack of hands. And those are just the people who call a lawyer. Most injured people never do. . But if injustice is administrative, then justice is an engineering problem. And engineering problems get solved. . . That's why Andco exists. We build agents that do the chasing: medical records, police reports, insurance verifications, demand drafts. . They can wait on hold infinitely. They don't lose the fax number. They work every case like it's the only one, at a scale no back office can match. . We started in pre-litigation because that's where cases are won, lost, or never started. We won't stop there. Everything standing between an injured person and being made whole is ours to fix. . The world we're building is easy to picture. Firms that never say no to someone they could win for. Demands out in weeks, not seasons. A system where the merits decide, not the paperwork. . We chase the paper so lawyers can chase justice. More cases taken. More people made whole. . . . . . . .
