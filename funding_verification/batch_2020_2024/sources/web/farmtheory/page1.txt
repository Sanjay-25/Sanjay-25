About — Farmtheory Skip to content Home About Contact Get in touch . About us Freeform in every kitchen, and a fully harvested field on every farm.. That is the whole idea. Everything we build works towards it.. . . . The problem. A third of the harvest never leaves the farm.. Buyers grade fruit and vegetables on appearance. Anything too small, too large, bent, split, rain-marked or scarred is rejected before it is ever tasted — around 30% of a farmer’s harvest , left in the field because there is nobody to sell it to.. None of it is spoiled. It is ordinary food that failed a cosmetic standard. We call it freeform produce, and buying it is the entire business.. . . . . What we do. We buy the whole field, and take it straight to the kitchen.. Conventional produce moves from the farmer to an agent, then a trader, then the mandi, then a wholesaler, and finally to a business. Each stop takes a margin and a day of freshness, and none of them will take the freeform grades. We replaced that chain with two steps of our own.. . Farm-level collection. We buy direct at collection centres near the farm, taking the full harvest rather than only the grades that look the part.. . Our distribution centre. Sorted, graded, weighed and packed in-house, with inventory and wastage tracked at every step.. . Straight to the business. Delivered on our own fleet in under 20 hours, with no wholesaler in between.. . . . . Who we serve. Kitchens that cook at volume.. Freeform works best where produce gets prepped rather than plated whole — which covers most of a professional kitchen’s volume.. . Restaurants & HoReCa. Single sites and multi-outlet chains, with every location on one account.. . Cloud kitchens. High-volume, delivery-first operations where food cost decides the margin.. . Food processing. Purées, sauces, dehydrated and ready-to-cook lines, where shape never survives the process.. . Catering & food service. Institutional kitchens and contract caterers buying to a fixed cost per head.. . . . . Impact. Three things happen at once.. The farmer sells more of what they grew. The kitchen pays less for it. And food that was going to rot gets eaten instead.. . ~20% more. Farmer income. Earned on produce that previously had no buyer at any price.. . 25–30%. Customer savings. Farmer livelihoods. Supplying us across multiple Indian states, with more added each season.. . . . . Sourcing produce, or growing it?.
