Public Safety Investments Drive This Week's SLED News. . . Blake Thorne. September 18, 2026. •. 5 min read. . . . . . . . . . . . . This Week’s Featured Headlines. 1. Mississippi Awards $104M Across 167 Rural Health Grants Rich with AI and Cybersecurity Investment . Source: Office of Governor Tate Reeves | September 14, 2026. Governor Tate Reeves announced 167 awards totaling more than $104 million through the Mississippi Rural Health Transformation Program, representing the first three grant opportunities in a five-year, $205.9 million statewide transformation effort.. ‍. What happened . Dozens of individual awards fund specific technology deployments, including Hattiesburg Clinic's $537,094 rollout of ambient AI documentation across roughly 500 providers, Sidhu Pediatrics' AI-enabled clinical documentation and population health platform, and multiple facilities investing in cybersecurity modernization spanning firewalls, SIEM monitoring, endpoint protection, and incident response planning.. Additional categories still to come include a workforce and psychiatric emergency services round expected within 30 to 45 days, plus an October launch focused specifically on helping rural providers modernize their electronic health record systems.. What it means for you . Health IT vendors, AI documentation platform providers, and cybersecurity firms serving small rural facilities should study this award list closely, since dozens of clinics and hospitals just received dedicated technology modernization budgets covering everything from telehealth carts to remote patient monitoring for chronic disease management.. With Mississippi's EHR-focused funding round opening in October and psychiatric and workforce awards landing within weeks, vendors positioned now with rural-appropriate, cybersecurity-hardened health technology have a rare multi-month runway of sequential funding opportunities in a single state.. 2. Texas Prepares Broad Statewide Cybersecurity Procurement Open to All Public Sector Buyers . Source: Government Technology | September 11, 2026. The Texas Department of Information Resources is preparing to release a wide-ranging cybersecurity procurement covering data security, encryption, biometric authentication, and threat monitoring, with resulting contracts available to state agencies, local governments, higher education institutions, and public schools statewide.. ‍. What happened . The solicitation refreshes cooperative contracts last awarded in fiscal year 2021 and spans an unusually broad category list including redaction and de-identification software, backup and recovery tools, firewalls, card-access security systems, and identity-theft protection services.. Vendor questions are due October 14, responses are due November 18, and DIR may make multiple awards through the process, with resulting agreements structured as indefinite-quantity contracts carrying no guaranteed minimum purchase.. What it means for you . Cybersecurity vendors across nearly every product category, from encryption software to biometric authentication hardware, should treat this as a near-term must-respond opportunity given the breadth of eligible public sector customers and the relatively short window before November's deadline.. Because the resulting cooperative contracts will be usable by any qualifying Texas public entity rather than a single agency, a successful award here could open the door to purchase orders from hundreds of eligible school districts, cities, counties, and universities without requiring a separate bid for each one.. 3. Minnesota Pilots New Technology to Combat Financial Aid Fraud in Public Colleges . Source: Governing (via MinnPost) | September 11, 2026. The Minnesota State system of 26 colleges and seven universities is preparing to pilot an automated identity verification system funded by a $3 million legislative appropriation this spring, aimed at combating so-called ghost students who steal financial aid using fake or stolen identities.. ‍. What happened . Minnesota State has already selected a vendor for the new systemwide software and plans to pilot it at a handful of schools before a full rollout, after years of individual campuses relying on their own patchwork solutions and manual staff review.. California offers a preview of the scale this problem can reach, having spent more than $150 million since 2022 on similar identity verification technology across its 116-college community college system, with California reporting $1.5 million lost to fraud this spring alone despite that investment.. What it means for you . Identity verification vendors and fraud detection platform providers should note that Minnesota's selected vendor is not yet public, meaning competitors focused on higher education fraud prevention still have a window to position for the eventual systemwide rollout beyond the initial pilot campuses.. States beyond Minnesota and California are almost certainly watching this rollout closely, since the ghost student problem is described as a fast-growing national issue, and vendors with proven higher education fraud detection technology should expect similar procurement conversations to open in other states over the coming year.. 4. Arizona State University Receives $2M to Develop Water Reuse Technology for Semiconductor Manufacturing . Source: KTAR News | September 16, 2026. Arizona State University received more than $2 million in federal funding from the National Institute of Standards and Technology to develop and certify new water reuse technology for semiconductor manufacturing at its Macro Technology Works facility in Tempe.. ‍. What happened . The two-phase project will first integrate early-stage water treatment technologies into a mobile, pilot-scale testing environment before validating them under manufacturing-representative conditions at the Tempe facility, with quarterly stakeholder reviews determining which technologies advance to each phase.. Arizona's congressional delegation secured the funding specifically to help the semiconductor industry expand chip production in the state without straining local water resources, positioning ASU's research as a bridge between early water-saving concepts and commercial-ready deployment.. What it means for you . Water treatment technology developers and semiconductor industry suppliers should view ASU's Macro Technology Works as an active testbed where new solutions can be validated and certified for commercial use, creating a credible proving ground before approaching chip manufacturers directly.. The project's quarterly stakeholder review structure means there will be multiple entry points over the life of the grant for vendors to introduce new water reuse technologies for consideration, rather than a single closed application window.. 5. Charleston County Moves to Approve $1.9M in Drone and Detection Technology for Public Safety . Source: ABC News | September 16, 2026. Charleston County is set to receive nearly $1.9 million in military grant funding through a partnership with Joint Base Lindsey Graham to purchase 20 new drones and drone detection technology, pending formal approval by the county Finance Committee next week.. ‍. What happened . The bulk of the funding will buy 20 new drones for emergency response uses including wildfire surveying, locating missing people during floods, and providing safety support at large public events, replacing the county's current fleet of DJI drones.. A separate portion funds drone detection systems combining hardware and software to identify and classify drones in the vicinity as friendly or unfriendly, a capability that officials say will be used primarily by state law enforcement rather than the county's own emergency management team.. What it means for you . Drone manufacturers and counter-drone detection vendors should watch for the Finance Committee's approval next week, since county officials have not yet finalized which specific drone or detection systems they intend to purchase, leaving the field open ahead of procurement.. County officials emphasized that current drone technology changes quickly and that no deployment timeline exists yet, suggesting there is still room for vendors to make the case for their systems before Charleston County locks in a purchasing decision..
