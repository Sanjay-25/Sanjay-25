Identify which link failed before contacting anyone: employer cutoff, ACH settlement , or bank posting. Payroll must transmit the file, the ACH network must settle the credit, and your bank must post or release the funds. A delay at any one link leaves your balance unchanged.. Direct deposits normally move through ACH. Credits may post within hours on the same business day, or settle one to two business days later. Weekends and federal holidays do not count as ACH settlement days. A file sent near a cutoff can miss the current processing window and arrive on the next banking day. The basic payment flow and timing rules are outlined in Nacha's ACH overview .. Practical rule: A zero balance does not prove payroll failed. A pending deposit does not prove the bank made a mistake. Your screen shows which question to ask next.. Use the visible evidence:. No pending transaction: Ask payroll whether the file was submitted before calling the bank.. Pending credit: Ask the bank whether the ACH arrived but remains unreleased.. Partial payment: Check for holds, split deposits, or payroll corrections.. Unexpected account details: Confirm the routing and account numbers with payroll.. Rent, childcare, and utility bills make the wait stressful, but the money usually has not vanished. The failure is generally a specific bottleneck in the chain. Confirm the employer submission first, then check settlement status, and finally ask the bank about posting or availability. If a gap remains, Gerald's free bank transfer can serve as a temporary bridge while the responsible party corrects the delay.. . What Happens Before Your Employer Hits Submit . Your bank can't post money that your employer hasn't transmitted. That sounds obvious, but it's the most commonly skipped check when someone asks, “Why hasn't my direct deposit hit?”. Payroll teams work against a provider deadline. The exact cutoff depends on the employer and payroll company, and a late submission can push the file into the next banking window. A weekend or federal holiday can create another interruption because ACH settlement doesn't run on those non-business days.. Small employers may run payroll manually, while larger employers often use a payroll provider with a fixed batch schedule. Either way, the key event is the same: the employer must approve and submit the payroll file . Until that happens, no receiving bank can locate or release your paycheck.. . Ask payroll for proof, not reassurance. Contact HR, payroll, or the person who submits wages. Ask for specific operational details:. Was this pay batch submitted? . What settlement date was assigned? . What routing and account numbers are on file? . Was your account added or changed after payroll processing began? . Was your first deposit delayed by employee onboarding or account verification? . Don't accept “it should be there” as an answer. You need confirmation that the file was transmitted and that your banking details match.. If payroll can't provide a submission confirmation or expected settlement date, treat the employer link as the leading suspect.. . How ACH Settlement Moves the Money . A direct deposit moves through three links: employer cutoff, ACH settlement, and bank posting. A break at any one of them explains why your payday balance can still read zero.. ACH runs in batches, not as a continuous instant-payment rail. Your employer or payroll provider creates the payment entries. The originating bank submits them to an ACH operator, and the receiving bank retrieves the processed file before crediting your account.. Timing depends on the submission window and the settlement service. ACH credits may settle on the same business day or be scheduled one or more business days ahead. Same Day ACH applies when the payment meets the relevant same-day cutoff. As noted in the opening section, Nacha's explanation of ACH processing outlines the network's operating framework.. . . . Why a Friday file can become a Tuesday credit. Payroll submitted Friday afternoon can miss that day's processing window. Saturday and Sunday do not provide ACH settlement, so Monday may be the next processing opportunity. If the receiving bank misses its first posting run, Tuesday becomes the first realistic day the funds appear as available.. That calendar pattern does not prove payroll made an error. A federal holiday can shift the schedule, while a bank outage or maintenance window can add another delay.. Settlement and posting are separate steps. One bank may display the credit quickly, while another waits for a scheduled posting cycle or additional verification. Use Gerald's banking and payment guidance to identify whether the break occurred during settlement or account posting.. Ask the right party the right question. Payroll confirms whether the file was submitted and assigned a settlement date. Your bank confirms whether it received the entry and whether it has posted the credit. If payroll confirms submission but the bank has no entry, the settlement link failed. If the bank received it but your balance remains unchanged, investigate posting. That diagnosis tells you whether to wait, call payroll, contact the bank, or use Gerald's free bank transfer as a short-term bridge.. . Why Your Bank May Be Holding the Funds . Sometimes the ACH credit has reached your bank, but your available balance still shows nothing. That's a posting problem , not necessarily a missing-payment problem.. Under Regulation CC, consumer ACH credit funds are generally required to be available by the next business day after the bank receives them. Banks can apply longer holds in edge cases involving new accounts, repeated overdrafts, large deposits, or other collectibility concerns. The distinction between a transaction being posted and money being available is central to understanding an apparent delay. This ACH hold explanation covers the availability issue and common reasons funds may remain restricted.. . Posted balance versus available balance. Your app may show several different states:. Received: The bank has identified an incoming credit.. Pending: The transaction is in the bank's processing queue.. Posted: The credit appears in account history.. Available: The funds can be spent or withdrawn.. These labels aren't interchangeable. A bank can receive the file, record the transaction, and still restrict the available balance while its system completes verification.. Call the deposit-support line or branch if the pending status lasts beyond the timeline in your account agreement. Ask three direct questions:. Has the bank received an ACH credit from my employer?. Is there a hold or verification review on the transaction?. What date and time will the funds become available under the bank's policy?. Don't ask only whether the money is “there.” Ask whether it's received, posted, or available . That vocabulary forces the representative to check the right system record.. For payroll specifically, Nacha says employees' direct-deposit wages are available by 9 a.m. on payday in virtually all cases, and a qualifying Same Day ACH payroll submitted within the same-day windows can be available by 5 p.m. on Friday. Those are operational benchmarks, not guarantees that every bank will display every deposit at the same moment.. . Match the Symptom to the Likely Cause . The Action Ladder to Run in Order . Run these checks sequentially. Calling payroll, your bank, and a payment app at the same time creates noise, and each party will send you back to the other without a transaction reference.. . Start with your own account screen. Refresh the transaction list and open the balance detail. Look for a pending ACH credit, a posted credit that isn't available, or an alert about verification. Take a screenshot showing the date, balance, and status.. . Check the payroll record. Open your employer's payroll portal if you have access. Confirm the pay date, payment method, destination account, and submission status. If the portal shows a trace number or payment reference, save it before you call the bank.. . Call the bank's deposit team. Give the representative the employer name, expected pay date, amount, and payroll reference. Ask whether an inbound ACH exists and whether a hold is blocking availability. A general customer-service agent may not have the same visibility as the deposit or ACH support team, so request the correct department.. . Return to payroll when the bank finds nothing. If the bank confirms there's no inbound ACH entry, contact HR or payroll immediately. Ask them to verify the file, account details, settlement date, and whether the payment was rejected or redirected.. . Keep a clean record. Write down call times, representative names, reference numbers, and promised follow-up dates. Keep screenshots and payroll statements. If the issue becomes a wage complaint or formal escalation, a clear timeline is more useful than a folder full of frustrated messages.. Do this in order: app status, payroll record, bank trace, employer escalation, documentation.. . Bridging the Gap While You Wait .
