Health data events processed this year. . . . . . . Frequently asked questions. What is Terra API? Terra is a health and fitness data API that gives developers a single integration to connect apps with wearables, health apps, medical devices, and sensors. One API call returns standardised data from 500+ providers, including Garmin, Fitbit, Apple Health, Oura, WHOOP and Strava, without writing separate authentication flows or data parsers. Which wearables and health apps does Terra support? Terra connects to 500+ health data sources including wearables (Garmin, Fitbit, Apple Watch, Oura Ring, WHOOP, Polar, Samsung Health, Withings, Suunto, Wahoo), fitness apps (Strava, MyFitnessPal, Cronometer), blood testing providers, CGM devices, medical devices, and sensors. What health data types can I access through one API? Terra returns standardised activity, sleep, body and nutrition data. That covers sleep stages and latency, workouts, heart rate, HRV, VO2max, blood oxygen (SpO2), continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), menstrual cycle, body composition and per-set strength training. The full data type reference is at docs.tryterra.co. Is Terra HIPAA and GDPR compliant? Yes. Terra is HIPAA compliant, GDPR compliant, and SOC 2 Type II certified. All health data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Terra is built for healthcare applications, clinical research, insurance, and enterprise health platforms. How much does a wearable data API cost? Terra uses usage-based pricing, so you only pay for the active users you have. Plans start at $399 per month on the annual plan. All data types and integrations are included with no per-endpoint fee. See tryterra.co/pricing for full details. Is there a single API for all wearables? No. Terra's single API normalises data across all 500+ supported providers. Integrate once and gain access to data from Garmin, Fitbit, Apple Health, Oura, WHOOP, Strava and hundreds more, with a consistent data schema regardless of the source. How do I stream real-time heart rate from a wearable? Yes, through the Streaming API, which is a paid add-on to your plan. It delivers metrics on roughly a per-second basis, including heart rate, steps and distance, from BLE and ANT+ devices and phone sensors. Your mobile app forwards the stream to Terra using a Terra RT SDK, and your backend consumes it over a WebSocket. Is there an MCP server for wearable health data? Yes, through the AI Interface, an MCP server your agent connects to. It reads a user's health data at runtime, so the agent can request specific datasets, aggregated stats, trends and baselines without you calling the API, handling webhooks, or storing and parsing data yourself. Read access only. Can I send wearable data straight to my database? Terra delivers to webhooks, SQL databases (Postgres and MySQL), Supabase, cloud storage (S3, GCS and Azure Blob), queueing services (SQS and Kafka), MongoDB and Firestore. Destinations are configured per data source, so payloads can land straight in your database without you running a webhook handler. Can I push a structured workout to a Garmin watch? Yes, for providers that support it. Terra's write endpoints create a bi-directional stream between your app and connected integrations, and the Planned Workouts add-on pushes structured runs, rides, swims and strength blocks to Garmin, Coros, Hammerhead and TodaysPlan, and to Apple Watch through the mobile SDK. How do I connect to the Garmin API? Garmin has paused new applications to its Connect Developer Program while it completes updates, and has not announced a reopening date, so there is currently no channel to apply for direct access. Terra has maintained a Garmin integration for years as an established partner, so Garmin data flows through Terra today. Users still grant consent through Garmin's own authorization flow. Which Garmin data can I get through Terra? Activity, sleep, heart rate, body metrics and daily health stats, plus Garmin's own composite metrics (Body Battery, Stress and Training Effect) with per-minute detail where Garmin provides it. Garmin's branding requirements apply to what you build with it, including dashboards, exports, and derived or AI-generated outputs. . . . . . . . . . . .
