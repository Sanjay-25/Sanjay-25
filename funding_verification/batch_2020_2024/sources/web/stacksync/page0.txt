Stacksync connects the systems a company runs on: CRMs such as Salesforce and HubSpot, ERPs such as NetSuite and SAP, and databases such as Postgres. You map fields once and choose which side may write each one. Stacksync then keeps records in step through two-way sync, and the other modules build on that same data. . . Two-way sync Real-time sync between CRMs, ERPs and databases, with write direction set per field.. . Workflow automation Trigger actions on record changes, configured in the builder or as YAML/JSON files.. . Event queues Process the events that move between your systems in order, with automatic retries.. . Database hosting Managed databases that sit next to your synced data.. . AI agents Agents that read and write the same live data your syncs maintain.. . EDI Trading-partner EDI connected to the ERP and commerce systems you already run.. . . Where Stacksync fits. Buyers compare Stacksync with four groups of tools. iPaaS vendors such as Celigo, Workato, Boomi and MuleSoft build integrations as step-by-step flows. Heroku Connect syncs Salesforce with Heroku Postgres. ELT tools such as Fivetran copy data one way into a warehouse. EDI networks such as SPS Commerce and TrueCommerce exchange documents with trading partners. Stacksync covers the operational side: live two-way sync between business systems, with workflows and EDI on top. .
