Our hedge fund-in-a-box offering is focused on the most traditionally “common” hedge fund types that will primarily engage in trading the following types of assets:. On-exchange securities (e.g. equities, options, ETFs, bonds, warrants), including fixed income. On-exchange derivatives (e.g. futures, forex). Funds of funds (e.g. investing into other private funds). Additionally, and so long as it is not the primary purpose of the fund, funds that trade the above but also wish to have a limited amount of side-pocketing/general exposure to the following are also supported:. Certain private securities (e.g. other private funds or certain venture/private stock side pockets). Certain spot cryptocurrencies (no crypto derivatives, staking, yield farming, etc) on major institutional crypto primes only.. NOTE: We do not support the following:. Crypto-only funds or funds that wish to trade on DEXs, offshore exchanges, and/or engage in yield farming or staking.. Real estate or private equity funds. VC funds (unless in the context of side-pocketing in an otherwise non-VC fund). Physical commodities funds. Alternative asset funds (e.g. energy grids, debt, gambling, etc). If you aren’t sure, simply reach out here and a member of our team will review and let you know if we can support your fund.. . . . Do I need FINRA licenses and/or to be a "RIA" to launch a fund? . Short answer: Quite possibly not, and most emerging fund managers do not have any licensure, but it depends.. Longer answer: FINRA licensure requirements for securities-only hedge funds – typically the Series 65 – come from being a pre-requisite to being able to register as a “registered investment adviser” (aka “RIA”) at the state level. However, most emerging managers would operate instead as an excluded or “exempt reporting adviser” (aka “ERA”). Amongst other things, ERAs do not need any FINRA licensure.. Most states have an ERA “framework” wherein, subject to certain requirements and limitations, advisers to private funds can operate as ERAs. Note that which state(s) have jurisidiction is simply based on where you (and other key personnel) are physically located; you cannot electively “opt out” of your state’s laws into a more favorable one unless you are actually there, just as you cannot opt out of taxes in the state you’re based in and choose a more tax favorable state (that’d be nice, though).. The ERA framework varies state-to-state but generally entails:. You (and your team, if any) can only engage in investment advisory activity relating to private funds;. Investors in the private fund(s) must be limited to “qualified clients” (generally, a net worth/net assets of $2.2m), which is a higher standard than “accredited investor”. There are a handful of states (New York, Florida, Ohio, Georgia, Connecticut, as examples) wherein investors can just be accredited without also being qualified clients, but this is less common.. Common states where ERA-related requirements require investors to be qualified clients in order to invest in a hedge fund and be charged performance fees include California, Texas, and Massachussetts.. Note that this is based on where the adviser is, not where the investors are.. . You (and your team, if any) have less than $150m in assets under management in the private fund(s). Lastly, it is quite common that the fund would be required to receive a third-party, annual audit, which is mostly a cost consideration (a “cheap” audit would be in the $15-20k/year ballpark). Repool can provide you further information on the matter, but note that we cannot actually make a judgement or call as to whether you should or should not/can or cannot be an ERA or RIA – you need to make that determination yourself.. . . . Repool works with fund managers of all AUMs; however, it is fair to say that our most typical customers are what the SEC would regulatorily classify as “small” or “mid-size” advisers (sub $25m and $25-100m, respectively). That said, Repool does and can work with larger managers, but the correlative reason it is less common is principally asset related; larger funds are more likely to engage in trading assets Repool does not support for its fund launch solutions (see above FAQ for more info).. Here are some thoughts based on projected fund size/soft commitments: . First – as a general note, after working with dozens of funds, it is almost always the case that managers raise less than they expect/less than they have soft-committed. We recommend you set your expectations accordingly.. Below $2m: Unfortunately, you are likely too small to sustain a fund. We recommend continuing to build a track record and fundraising.. $2m – 5m : This is “on the edge” – viable, and many large funds started at this size, but do have clear expectations: This fund size is simply too small to likely persist more than a year or two without growth, and certainly doesn’t generate meaningful income for the manager.. We recommend that such funds have clear plans about how long they are willing to run at this size and what their plans are over the 1-3 year period if they fail to grow further.. . $5 – 25m : Solid fund size and a lot of emerging funds hover around here indefinitely and/or seek to break out from here. This is operationally feasible full-time for an indefinite period. Funds in this range are generally still solo or two-person shops only. . $25m+ : Above emerging manager average and high potential.. . . . . What are common restrictions for hedge funds? . This is only a general, informational guideline, but at minimum , most hedge funds are structured in ways where the following restrictions from various securities regulatory acts will apply:. – 100 or fewer investors. – Investors must all be at least accredited. – No general solicitation. Depending on your location or facts and circumstances, there may be additional restrictions alongside or on top of these limitations.
