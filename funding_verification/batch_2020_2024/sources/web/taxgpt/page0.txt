My client is a two-partner LLC in California with $900K of net income. Would a PTET election reduce their federal tax, and what are the deadlines?. . A. Yes, a California PTE elective tax election can reduce their federal tax, but the benefit is smaller than it used to be, and the deadline depends on which year you're asking about.. Why it works. Not because of a trade-or-business exception to the SALT cap. That rationale is wrong. §164(b)(6)'s carve-out covers only real and personal property taxes, plus foreign income taxes. Domestic state income taxes get no such relief at the individual level. It works because the elective tax is imposed on and paid by the LLC, so it reduces ordinary income on the K-1 and never reaches the partner's §164 computation at all. There's nothing left to cap. That's the Notice 2020-75 reasoning.. Critical timing point: Notice 2020-75 gives the deduction in the year of payment. To land it in the intended year, the LLC must pay the full elective tax by December 31, not by the return due date. Most commonly missed detail in this whole area.. The federal benefit now requires member-level modeling. OBBBA increased the individual SALT cap to $40,000 for 2025 and $40,400 for 2026, subject to a MAGI-based phase-down and $10,000 floor. A $900,000 LLC profit split equally does not by itself establish that either member is in the phase-down, because approximately $450,000 per member is below the 2026 $505,000 threshold; other household income could change that result. Compare the entity-level PTET deduction against the SALT deduction each member could otherwise claim, and also model any resulting reduction in the member's §199A deduction.. The deadlines. The June 15 prepayment rule is not uniform across 2022-2030:. 2022-2025: missing the June 15 payment (greater of $1,000 or 50% of prior-year elective tax) kills the election.. 2026-2030: SB 132 changed this. R&TC §19914(b) lets you elect anyway; §17052.11 just reduces each member's credit by 12.5% of their share of the unpaid amount. A penalty, not a disqualification.. Since we're past June 15, 2026, that distinction decides the case. Telling this client the window closed would be wrong.. The balance is due by the original return due date, without regard to extensions. But the election itself is made on a timely filed original return, and a return filed under extension still counts. So:. If this is TY2025, the June 15, 2025 payment was made, and the Form 568 is still unfiled, you have until October 15, 2026 to make the election. That's a live opportunity, not a missed one.. Sizing it. $900,000 × 9.3% = $83,700 is correct arithmetic on a base I don't trust yet. Qualified net income counts only consenting qualified taxpayers' shares and adds guaranteed payments back. If the $900K is stated after guaranteed payments, the base is higher. If either member is an entity rather than an individual or trust, that share drops out entirely. Also remember the $800 annual tax and the $2,500 LLC fee apply either way, the credit is nonrefundable with a 5-year carryforward, and the 9.3% flat rate doesn't cover a member's full 12.3%+ California liability.. Before this becomes advice, I need four things: the tax year, whether the June 15 prepayment was made and for how much, whether the 568 has been filed, and whether the $900K is before or after guaranteed payments.. . . .
